# Oddhaven: brainstorm

Status: raw ideas for the roast and plan steps. Nothing here is decided. Other games are named only as reference and
must never reach player-facing text. All names are working names.

Reference points from the brief: the Sims 3 (background houses keep living), RimWorld (autonomy), Tomodachi Life
(funny social moments), Wobbly Life (physical jobs), Satisfactory (trucks that reset onto their route).

---

## 1. The core loop

### 1.1 The hook in one line

**You are the town's most meddling house spirit: everyone gets on with their lives without you, and you can slip inside
any of them for as long as it is worth it, and they will have opinions about what you did with their body.**

The problem with "god mode or direct control" as a menu option is that it is two half-games sharing a save. People will
pick one and ignore the other. This brainstorm's central proposal is to make the switch itself the game:

- **Hands are the scarce resource.** A household of four has four lives running at once and you have one pair of hands.
  Everything you do not take over still happens, decently. So each day is a choice: *where is my one pair of hands
  worth the most right now?* That question is what a god game and a hands-on game have in common.
- **Delegated is fine, hands-on is special.** A simulated shift pays steady money and rarely goes wrong. A hands-on
  shift pays more and is the only way to get the good stuff: fast promotions, rare finds, saving someone, a story.
  It must never feel as if the simulation is punishing you for not playing (that turns it into chores).
- **Moments pull you in.** The game flags the 2-4 best things happening right now as a **Moment** with a short timer:
  "Fire at Dunmore Bakery. Your son is on shift. Take over?" Ignore it and the simulation resolves it, with a result
  you hear about later. Say yes and you drop straight into that character at that spot, no travel.
- **Bodies remember.** When you hand a character back, they get a "what did I just do" reaction. They wake up in a
  fountain, with a medal, or with a stranger's phone number. This is the comedy engine and it writes itself out of
  real game state.

### 1.2 The fiction that holds it together (optional but strong)

The player is **the Lodger**, a house spirit, which in this world is a known, slightly embarrassing thing to have, like
a mouse problem or a very persistent uncle. God view is the Lodger floating around. Taking over is "being Lodged".
Residents can talk about it: "I lost my whole Tuesday to the Lodger and came home a sergeant."

- Characters have a **Lodger Opinion** that moves with how you treated their body and life. Fans let you in for free.
  Someone you crashed three cars with resists: their autonomy keeps tugging against the stick for a few seconds.
- Co-op fits the fiction: a friend is a second Lodger in the same town. Permission to control a character is that
  household's say-so. A party leader taking control back is "the landlord's knock".
- A character splitting off into a new party is "moving out".

Nothing mechanical depends on this. If the owner hates the fiction, it reskins to a plain "follow" camera.

### 1.3 The layers

**Minute (the verb layer).** One clear physical thing: hold the hose on the flames, pull the lever when the log lines
up, carry the wobbling crate. Ten seconds to understand, satisfying to be good at. In god view the minute is a poke:
tap someone, suggest something, watch them misunderstand it.

**Day (the planning layer).** Target 12-15 real minutes at normal speed, and a phone session should end cleanly in 5.
- Morning: the household board shows today's shifts, appointments, needs, the weather, the Moments forecast.
- Spend your hands: pick one or two things to do yourself, leave the rest to run.
- Evening: **the Day's Mail**, a one-screen funny recap. Pay in, bills, who fell out with whom, what the Lodger's
  victims are saying. Ends with one hook for tomorrow ("Your neighbour has bought a suspiciously large shovel").
- A shift is a quota rather than a clock when you play it, so a hands-on shift is 2-4 real minutes, not eight hours.
  The world clock jumps forward at the end. A shift you abandon pays for the part you did.

**Week (the goal layer).**
- Friday is payday, Sunday is rent and bills. A town event every week, known in advance (market day, the road race,
  the Moon Fair, a wedding).
- **The Odd Sheet**, the weekly town paper, is generated from what actually happened: your crimes, your rescues, a
  column by the local gossip with a grudge against you. The front page is the best trophy in the game.
- Weekly household goal chosen from three cards: "get Bo promoted", "win the feud", "pay off the van".

**Long term (the why-am-I-here layer).**
- Each character has a **Life Dream** that shapes what they want (own the sawmill, marry the mayor, become Chief, get
  their dead aunt to forgive them). Fulfilling dreams earns Lodger Favour, used to unlock things.
- Own things: second house, a shop, the sawmill, a fleet of vans, then run for mayor.
- Town state changes because of you and it stays changed. The town's economy is real (see 3), so a family that runs
  the sawmill well changes what the shops sell and what houses cost.
- **The Town Book**: a permanent timeline of the town's history and a family tree. Generations if the plan wants
  them: kids grow up, and an old character can retire into a ghost relative (undead are a race here, so that is not
  even strange).

### 1.4 Why one more day?

1. Open thread: the recap always ends on something unresolved and specific to your save.
2. Money is always nearly enough: the next vehicle or house is one good shift away.
3. Curiosity about what the simulation did while you were not looking, which you only learn from the mail or by
   floating past.
4. A friend might have done something to your town overnight (co-op, later).

### 1.5 The real difference from the games it borrows from

| Game | What you do | What Oddhaven adds |
|---|---|---|
| Life sim | Manage needs from above | Drop in and physically do the job, then return |
| Colony sim | Keep people alive | Modern comedy, no survival pressure, a real road network |
| Physical job game | Do silly jobs by hand | A town that keeps working and remembers when you stop |
| Party/social toy | Watch tiny people be funny | Those funny moments have consequences for jobs and money |

## 2. Ideas (grouped, 84 in all)

### Social and funny moments
1. **Dream bubbles.** Sleeping characters show a dream made from their real memories (the dog, the promotion, the
   person they fancy). Tap to read it. Sometimes it foreshadows what they do tomorrow.
2. **Rap battles for arguments.** Two grudge-holders settle it with a voice-engine rhyme-off made from Lingo. The crowd
   votes. The robotic voice is the joke until the voice is swapped.
3. **Confession letters.** A character slips a love letter into the wrong letterbox. You can intercept, deliver or
   rewrite it.
4. **Bad advice column.** A character writes to you for advice. You pick an answer from three. They follow it. All
   three are terrible in different ways.
5. **Rumour mill.** Rumours are objects that travel person to person and mutate at each telling: "Bo was fired" becomes
   "Bo set fire to the mill" by Friday. Rumours can be traced back, planted or killed.
6. **Weddings that can go wrong.** Ex shows up, ring is lost (a mini chase), a giant has to duck under the arch, the
   officiant is a goblin with a very short licence.
7. **Feuds with history.** A feud has a stated cause and a score. It passes down through families. The Book records
   who started it, and the families disagree about that.
8. **Neighbour gifts.** Unwanted gifts get regifted around the town. A jar of pickles can complete a full circuit.
9. **Group chat.** The household has a group chat on an in-game phone: the voices of the Lingo system in text form, with
   typing bubbles, awkward photos and someone who always gets the wrong chat.
10. **Awkward encounters.** Your date is your boss. Your boss is the person you arrested yesterday.
11. **Secret friendships.** Two sworn enemies are found feeding the same stray at midnight.
12. **The Very Long Goodbye.** A halfling cannot leave a party in under forty minutes. It is simulated exactly.
13. **Pets that pick their owner.** A stray decides, you do not. Pets can be magical and slightly illegal.

### Needs and moods
14. Six shared needs: Food, Sleep, Fun, Company, Clean, Comfort. Keep it readable on a phone as one mood face with a
    tap to expand.
15. **One race need each**: a dwarf wants time underground or near stone, an elf wants something green nearby, a goblin
    wants to own something shiny, an orc wants to be loud, a giant wants room, a halfling wants snacks at odd hours, the
    undead want to feel remembered, a beast-folk wants a run outdoors, a human wants a plan for the weekend.
16. **Mood weather.** A very good or very bad mood leaves a small visible effect around a character: a tiny raincloud,
    a sparkle. Magic leaks when you are upset.
17. **Quirks not stats.** Traits read like people: "narrates own actions", "afraid of ducks", "tips far too much".
18. **Burnout.** Too many hands-on shifts without Fun, and a character takes a dramatic mental health day at the
    worst moment. Encourages spreading your attention.
19. **Autonomy dial.** One per character, from "does as told" to "their own person". High autonomy means better
    stories, more mischief.
20. **Good day streak.** Characters whose needs all stay up get a visible glow and a shift bonus (positive reward, no
    punishment for a bad day).

### Emergent stories
21. **The Lodger's Mess.** Anything you did in someone's body stays their problem: the shopping trolley still in the
    canal, the debt, the kiss.
22. **Witnesses.** Everything public has witnesses, and they are the rumour sources.
23. **Inheritance disputes** after a death, or after a very long nap by an undead relative.
24. **Runaway kid.** A teenager leaves home after a fight. They really are somewhere in the town, and the simulation
    keeps running their life.
25. **Unlikely hero.** The game spots when a low-status character does something big and gives them a parade.
26. **The Who's Who wall.** A relationships map that is itself fun to read, with drama lines in red.
27. **Second life.** The moment you stop caring about a family, they become ordinary townsfolk and you can bump into
    them years later.

### Town events
28. **Moon Fair.** Monthly: rides, cursed goldfish, tiny-stakes games made from reused job minigames.
29. **The Great Soapbox Race.** Town road race using the real vehicle physics.
30. **Mayoral election.** Real votes from the opinions of each simulated citizen. Bribes, posters, debates.
31. **Strike.** Workers at a business with low mood down tools. The picket line is a real physical line. Crossing it
    has social costs.
32. **Town meeting** where the funny Lingo argument decides something real (build the bridge? ban the ducks?).
33. **Bake-off, dog show, talent night, pumpkin weigh-in.** Cheap events using existing systems.
34. **Parade**, with floats built on real flatbed trucks that can go wrong.

### Magic in daily life
35. **Charms as appliances.** A warming charm instead of a heater, a charm fridge that has opinions. Charms wear out
    and need a repair call.
36. **Permit system.** Magic is licensed like driving: small spells free, big ones need a permit, so police have
    magic-misuse cases.
37. **Six household spells** on a character (one per slot on the phone wheel): Tidy, Warm, Fetch, Mend, Hush, Spark.
    Fast, cheap, and they go wrong in funny ways when the caster is in a bad mood.
38. **Broom commuter lane.** A real traffic lane above the road. Later expansion only.
39. **Enchanted cars.** Tune a car with magic parts: floats over puddles, hums show tunes, will not reverse.
40. **Tow gnomes.** The in-world reason NPC cars reset: a flipped car vanishes in a puff, reappears on the road, and
    its owner receives a small gnome invoice. Same rule as the Satisfactory-style trucks.
41. **Haunted appliances.** The undead race has distant relatives who live in your toaster.
42. **Potion cooking.** Normal cooking with odd ingredients gives temporary traits ("speaks only in questions").
43. **Curses from the small print.** Sign a bad contract with a goblin lender and a real curse applies until it is
    paid.

### Earning money, beyond a salary
44. **Own a shop**, then choose stock and prices. Supply comes from the real production chain.
45. **Gig app.** A phone app listing one-off jobs: carry a sofa, walk a dog, catch a chicken. Short, cheap, instant,
    good for a five-minute session.
46. **Flip vehicles.** Buy a wreck, fix it hands-on at the garage, sell it.
47. **Landlord.** Buy the second house, rent it to a simulated family. Hate being called about the plumbing.
48. **Fishing and foraging spots** paying into a market stall.
49. **Busking** with the voice system.
50. **Pawn the weird stuff** found while monster hunting.
51. **Insurance claims** (legitimate or suspicious).

### Crime and police
52. **Five crimes to begin with**: speeding, shoplifting, unlicensed magic, vandalism, car theft. Heist much later.
53. **Wanted works socially.** Police know your face, but so do your neighbours. Reputation, not a star meter.
54. **Court.** A short, funny trial using Lingo. Character witnesses are people you actually know. Bribing a giant is
    hard, because they keep saying it out loud.
55. **Jail** is a building you can enter, and a character there keeps living (and makes friends, or enemies).
56. **Parking tickets.** Yes, even from the Lodger. Especially from the Lodger.
57. **Choose a side.** The same crime can be played from either side by swapping characters.

### Disasters
58. **House fires** that really spread between rooms, simulated cheaply by the room rather than per cell.
59. **Flood on the river road**, closing the route and rerouting all simulated traffic.
60. **Power cut.** Every electric thing stops, and charm houses glow smugly.
61. **Rift in a basement.** Small monster incursion (monster hunter call-out).
62. **Runaway log truck** rolling down the hill.
63. **Storm** with fallen trees that block a road until forestry or a player shifts them.
64. **Mine collapse** with a real rescue job.
65. **Giant sneeze season.** A giant with a cold is a local weather event.

### Collecting
66. **Town Album.** A camera on the phone. Photographs of funny, rare or dangerous moments fill out a sticker book.
67. **Vehicle garage** as a collection, with every owned vehicle listed.
68. **Fish, bugs, rocks and small monsters** with a museum that takes donations.
69. **Mail-order oddities.** Weird items arrive in the post.
70. **Medals for silly stats**: "Most Fountains Entered", "Town's Worst Parker".
71. **Recipe cards** from every household's grandmother.

### Seasons
72. A year of four 7-day seasons (28 days), changing crops, fish, clothes, what people talk about.
73. **Autumn**: harvest job peak, the Moon Fair at its largest, undead family reunion.
74. **Winter**: snow plough job, sledding, icy roads, a giving-gifts week.
75. **Spring**: lambing, floods, the love season (more dates, more disasters).
76. **Summer**: tourists, lifeguard job, wildfire risk, the road race.
77. Real-date holidays follow the shared seasons table used by other games (Halloween in October).

### Co-op
78. **Rival households in one town.** Two friends, two families, one job vacancy.
79. **Babysitting.** Hand a friend your kid character with a permission setting and a look of concern.
80. **Night shift.** A friend can play your town's overnight jobs while you are offline.
81. **Crew jobs.** Fire engine, removal van and crane jobs are better with two players: one steers, one works the
    hose or the hook.
82. **Move out.** A friend's character splits into a new party and buys the house across the street, feud optional.

### Polish that adds a lot
83. **Vote on the ducks.** The town has ducks. They are a running thread through everything: traffic, weddings,
    elections. Every town needs one running joke.
84. **Replay of the day.** The recap can play a 10-second clip of the funniest physical moment with your friends.

## 3. Jobs

Rules for this table:
- **One verb** each, with a stick and at most two big buttons. Physical, slightly wobbly, funny when it fails.
- "Off-screen output" is what the simulation produces when nobody is playing it. Goods are real items that feed the
  town economy, so a simulated logger and a hands-on logger both put logs into the same mill.
- Pay is per shift at entry level, in dollars, with **simulated / best hands-on**. Starter weekly costs would be around
  $700 rent and bills. All numbers are placeholders for the tuning pass.
- Hands-on should pay about 1.5-2x, plus the things simulation cannot give (promotion speed, finds, Moments).

### Monster hunting (the combat job)

Monsters come from **rifts**, small tears that open in cellars, sewers, the Thicket and the old mine. It is a licensed
municipal job with a form to fill in afterwards.

Combat (phone): stick moves, one Strike button (three hit chain), one Dodge button, one wheel slot for a gadget or a
spell. Enemies have clear tells. Hit timing and spacing, not stats, decide most fights. A party can follow you in, and
each ally is a simulated fighter who can be swapped into, mid-fight, which makes the god/hands-on idea pay off in
combat: **swap to the character who is in the most danger**.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pest controller | Catch | Small monsters in homes: net, trap, "there is a mimic in my dishwasher" | Calls cleared, a small risk of escape | $110 / $230 |
| Rift hunter | Fight | Combat run through a small room set, close the rift at the end | Rifts closed, monster parts, injuries | $160 / $380 |
| Rift rescue | Escort | Bring lost people out with you. They panic, and a catch-up rule applies | Survivors, town gratitude | $150 / $350 |

## 4. Wiring: levers, buttons, plates, events and actions

### 4.1 The rule that matters

**Everything in a building that can be switched is one wiring system, and it is plain data.** A job machine is not
special code. A sawmill is a blade, a belt, a lever and some wires. This gives three benefits:

- A character controlled by the simulation can use the same machine. It uses a lever as an object: "stand here, spend
  3 seconds, pull". There is no second copy of the rules.
- If a building is off-screen, its wiring does not run. The simulation just takes the job's summary result. When you
  walk in, it is in a sensible state.
- New job locations can be authored without new code (data only), and the phone stays cheap since most parts do nothing
  until triggered.

### 4.2 Vocabulary (small on purpose)

**Things that send a signal**
- **Lever** (on/off, stays), **Button** (a short pulse), **Pressure plate** (on while something heavy enough stands on it),
  **Trigger zone** (a person, vehicle or item enters), **Keypad or keycard**, **Item slot** (put the right thing in),
  **Timer** (each N seconds, or at a clock time), **Meter** (value passes a level: heat, water, pressure), **Spell mark**
  (reacts to a type of magic).

**Things that combine signals**
- **All of** (every input on), **Any of**, **Not**, **Latch** (once on stays on), **Delay**, **Count to N**, **Sequence**
  (must arrive in order).

**Things that receive a signal**
- **Door or gate**, **Light or sign**, **Belt or motor**, **Blade or press**, **Lift or crane**, **Valve**, **Spawn** (item or
  person), **Sound or alarm**, **Say** (a Lingo line), **Give** (money, item, goal progress), **Start event** (fire, rift, Moment),
  **Change meter**.

**Events and actions** are the same idea at the world scale. An *event* has a condition and a list of *actions*:
"at 18:00, if the shop is open, then close the shutter, turn off the sign, send staff home". The town's timetable, jobs
and disasters all run on this one list.

Sketch of a data row (format is for the plan to decide):

```
{ "id": "mill.feed", "when": { "allOf": ["mill.lever", "mill.plate.log"] }, "do": [["belt.start","b1"],["say","mill.running"]] }
```

### 4.3 Ten uses

1. **Sawmill line.** A log on the plate plus the lever on starts the belt; a light shows when the blade is at speed.
2. **Fire station.** Alarm event opens the bay doors, lights the pole light, starts the "get to the truck" trigger zone.
3. **Bank vault** (later crime). Two keys held at once at two different levers, so a heist needs two people.
4. **Quarry blast.** Timer plus siren, plunger only works when the trigger zone is clear, or it is your funeral.
5. **Train crossing.** A plate on the track drops the barrier and flashes the lights; NPC cars read the barrier as a stop.
6. **Shop front door.** Opening hours event unlocks it; a plate on the mat rings the bell and calls staff to the till.
7. **Sluice gate.** Two valves in sequence hold the river back during a flood, with a Meter driving the alarm.
8. **Secret basement.** Pull the odd candle holder, and the bookcase moves. Spell mark version for a hidden witch's room.
9. **Monster room puzzle.** Stand allies on plates to keep a gate open. Party members follow "stand here" commands.
10. **Elevator and lift bay.** A mechanic's car lift, a call button, a Latch for the safety lock; a broken wire is itself
    an electrician's job.

Player-made wiring is a future expansion (see 5), tied to house building.

## 5. Future iterations

The brief's world: two cities with the radius and distance of Thousandvale, later several, later the planet. The plan
should keep one rule from the start: **the world is a set of places joined by routes, and characters beyond a place's
detail level are only a schedule and some numbers.** That is what lets the map grow without a rewrite.

**Expansion 0: The small world.** Oddhaven (river port, shops, town hall, fire and police) and Brackenford (mills, mine,
quarry, farms), with one main road between them and forest, farmland, a lake and the old mine around. Four households
of real interest, about 60 simulated citizens, 12 jobs fully hands-on.

**Expansion 1: Filling in.** All jobs in section 3 except rail. The rest of the vehicles. Second house, shops you can
own. Seasons. A dozen town events.

**Expansion 2: Co-op proper.** Online parties, permissions, splitting, rival households, friends' towns, the night shift.

**Expansion 3: Custom worlds.** World picker: premade or a seeded random town. Map data is the same format, so the
seeded generator (using `proctown/`) produces towns the same way.

**Expansion 4: Build your home.** The owner's "not yet": build and decorate houses, then wire your own (player levers,
plates and traps). Could include a garage workshop for custom vehicles.

**Expansion 5: The county.** A third town, a highway, trains. Train driver job. Town rivalry events, county fair. Commuting
is real, so moving house changes your life.

**Expansion 6: A city.** The first dense city with districts, apartment towers, subway, a stadium, skyscraper fires, big
company jobs, an airport as a place. Traffic is abstracted further out. Business ownership grows to chains.

**Expansion 7: Coast and sea.** Islands, cargo ships, a navy-ish coastguard, deep-sea fishing, offshore rig, undersea
rifts. Ferries become routes.

**Expansion 8: The race homelands.** Elf forest republic, dwarf under-city, goblin free market, giant highlands, each
with a different culture, buildings and laws. Tourism. Language jokes.

**Expansion 9: The planet.** Flights to several regions using `worldgen/` and an airport network. Regions are simulated at
the level of numbers until you land. Travel is a real decision: your family goes on with life while you are away.

**Expansion 10: The Great Rift.** A world-scale event in which all the town lives you have built get involved. Season-based
story chapters, each with a new monster family and a new job.

## 6. Notes for the roast step

Things that seem most likely to go wrong, listed so they can be attacked:

- **Scope.** The full job table is about 55 jobs. Each is a physics minigame. Twelve great ones beat fifty dull ones. A
  shared kit (carry, aim, tension meter, stack, rhythm, drive) should cover most of them, and the "best 12" should be
  chosen by feel.
- **Phone cost.** Rapier in the browser, Chibi 2.5 characters and traffic on the same phone. Only near vehicles should be
  physical. Everything further out is points moving along road lines (the reset rule works naturally there).
- **Attention pacing.** If Moments interrupt too often it nags. Budget them: about 2-4 a day, always ignorable.
- **Sim fairness.** Simulated work should not be strictly worse or hands-on turns into a chore, but hands-on must still
  feel worth it. The cap on rank (not on income) is one answer.
- **Voice.** Hundreds of lines through the formant voice can grate. The swap point must be one module, as the brief says,
  and short barks matter more than long lines.
- **The Lodger fiction.** It may be too cute for some players. It costs little and can be reduced to flavour.
- **Tone with races and crime.** Keep it warm and silly. Racial jokes aim at the job, the habit or the bureaucracy,
  never at people being lesser.
- **The brief was cut off** at "PLAN THE". The plan should say so, and assume it meant "plan the milestones".

### Suggested smallest slice that proves the hook

One household in one town, one fire, one sawmill and one Moment. The test: float above the town, see a Moment, drop
into a firefighter, put out the fire, hand back, and read a funny recap that mentions it. If that loop is fun, the rest
is more of the same.
